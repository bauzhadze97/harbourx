Authenticator

A code from your phone on top of your password. It is the single most useful thing you can do for this account.

Password and sessions →
!
Two-factor is off Your password is the only thing protecting this account.
1

Get an authenticator app

Any of them work — Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy, 1Password, Bitwarden. If your password manager already offers this, use that one: the codes travel with you to a new phone.

2

Scan this with it

Point the app at the square. If you are reading this on the same phone the app is on, type the key underneath instead.

HarbourX · your account
—
3

Enter the code it shows you

Six digits, and it changes every thirty seconds. If it is refused, wait for the next one — a phone clock that is a minute out is the usual reason.

Once it is on, signing in asks for a six-digit code as well as your password. Somebody who has your password still cannot get in.

Save your backup codes

Each one works once, in place of a code from the app. They are how you get in when your phone is lost, broken or flat.

    !
    This is the only time these are shown. HarbourX keeps only a one-way hash of them, so we cannot read them back to you — not over the phone, not by email, not ever. Put them somewhere you will still have them when your phone is not in your hand.
    ↻

    New backup codes

    Issuing a new set immediately retires the old one. Do it if you have used most of them, or if you think the list has been seen.

    ✕

    Turn it off

    Your account goes back to a password alone. Enter a current code, or one of your backup codes.

    Why this matters here

    This account can move money. A password on its own is one guess, one leak or one reused login away from somebody else holding it.

    • ◷A code is good for thirty seconds and for one sign-in. Even captured, it is spent.
    • ⎋The secret is generated by HarbourX and shown to you once. It never leaves your account.
    • ▤Backup codes are stored only as hashes. Nobody at HarbourX can read them back.
    • ✉We will never ask for a code by email, phone or chat. Anyone who does is not us.

    If you lose your phone

    Sign in with one of your backup codes, turn two-factor off on this page, and set it up again on the new phone. If the codes are gone too, open a support ticket — identity checks apply before anything is reset.
